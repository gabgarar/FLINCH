# References

## Flares in Sun-as-a-star spectra and velocities

- Pietrow, A. G. M., et al. (2024). A comparative study of two X2.2 and X9.3 solar flares
  observed with HARPS-N. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 682, A46.
- Dumusque, X., et al. (2026). A decade of solar high-fidelity spectroscopy and precise radial
  velocities from HARPS-N. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 706, A231. The data release used
  here as second instrument. It cites Pietrow et al. (2024) as having shown that flares
  contribute negligibly to the velocity of quiet Sun-like stars; their limit was 50 cm/s,
  more than five times the signal of the Earth.
- Dumusque, X., et al. (2021). Three years of HARPS-N high-resolution spectroscopy and
  precise radial velocity data for the Sun. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*.
- Ma, Y. L., et al. (2024). Sun-as-a-star study of an X-class solar flare with spectroscopic
  observations of CHASE. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 966, 45.
- Otsu, T., et al. (2022). Sun-as-a-star analyses of various solar active events using
  H alpha spectral images taken by SMART/SDDI. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 939, 98.
- Otsu, T., et al. (2024). Sun-as-a-star analysis of the X1.6 flare on 2023 August 5:
  dynamics of postflare loops in spatially integrated observational data. *The Astrophysical
  Journal Letters*, 974, L13.
- Otsu, T., et al. (2026). Statistical study of the timing of the appearance of H alpha
  postflare loops: simple scaling law based on radiative cooling. *The Astrophysical
  Journal*, 997, 286.
- Liu, X., et al. (2025). Sun-as-a-star analysis of the solar eruption source region using
  H alpha spectroscopic observations of CHASE. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 993, 126.
- Yu, H. C., et al. (2025). Sun-as-a-star analysis of simulated solar flares. *Astronomy &
  Astrophysics*, 694, A315.
- De Wilde, M., et al. (2025). Synthesizing Sun-as-a-star flare spectra from high-resolution
  solar observations. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 700, A275.
- Zhang, J., et al. (2026). Can we distinguish the source region location of
  filament/prominence eruptions from the Sun-as-a-star H alpha spectrum? *The Astrophysical
  Journal*, 1004, 66.
- Xu, Y., et al. (2022). Sun-as-a-star spectroscopic observations of the line-of-sight
  velocity of a solar eruption on 2021 October 28. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 931, 76.
- Lu, H.-P., et al. (2023). Full velocities and propagation directions of coronal mass
  ejections inferred from simultaneous full-disk imaging and Sun-as-a-star spectroscopic
  observations. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 953, 68.

These are studies of single events, of a few events or of simulations, most of them in
H alpha from imaging spectrographs: the spectra are integrated over a region around the flare
and normalised by the light of the disc. Otsu et al. (2026) gather 34 flares, but measure in
images when the postflare loops appear. Only the first of the list above measures radial
velocities, for two flares: an upper limit of 50 cm/s and a request for a statistical
follow-up. The last two measure, in extreme-ultraviolet lines, the velocity of the material
ejected in eruptions (about 400 km/s along the line of sight in the event of Xu et al., and
eight ejections in twelve years of data in Lu et al.). That is the velocity of the ejecta,
not of the photospheric spectrum from which the radial velocity of a star is measured.

- Kumar, B., et al. (2010). On the flare induced high-frequency global waves in the Sun.

- Kumar, B., et al. (2017). On the signatures of flare-induced global waves in the Sun: GOLF

  high-frequency global oscillations. *Solar Physics*, 281, 21.

## Velocities inside the flaring area

These works look at the velocity where the flare is, in resolved images or in simulations, not
at the velocity of the whole disc.

- Saar, S. H., Palumbo, M. L., Haywood, R. D., & Dupree, A. K. (2018). A first look at the
  effect of flares on radial velocity jitter in G dwarfs: a punch and a splash? Poster at
  Cool Stars 20, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.1490103](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1490103).
  Pixels flagged as flares in SDO images of 2014 are shifted by 130 to 220 m/s with respect
  to the quiet Sun and cover 0.04 % of the disc.
- Monson, A. J., et al. (2021). Flare-induced photospheric velocity diagnostics. *The
  Astrophysical Journal*, 915, 16. Simulations: chromospheric emission makes photospheric
  Fe I lines look shifted by up to 40 m/s.

## Solar rotation

- Snodgrass, H. B., & Ulrich, R. K. (1990). Rotation of Doppler features in the solar
  photosphere. *The Astrophysical Journal*, 351, 309.

## Data

- **NEID solar data.** NEID Archive at the NASA Exoplanet Science Institute (NExScI),
  <https://neid.ipac.caltech.edu>.
- **HARPS-N solar data.** Ten-year data release on the DACE platform of the University of
  Geneva, <https://dace.unige.ch/sun/>.
- **GOES-R XRS.** Science-quality level-2 products of the NOAA National Centers for
  Environmental Information,
  <https://data.ngdc.noaa.gov/platforms/solar-space-observing-satellites/goes/>.
